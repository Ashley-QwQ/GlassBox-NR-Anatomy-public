## What This Page Covers

- What it is: The [binary32](APPENDIX_beginner_glossary.md#g056) computations, format carriers, and [publication](APPENDIX_beginner_glossary.md#g091) boundaries that actually appear in the default 158-[launch](APPENDIX_beginner_glossary.md#g080) chain; F32 is not a network-wide uniform accumulation precision.
- Input → output: Expanded [half](APPENDIX_beginner_glossary.md#g054) values, parameter/[resource](APPENDIX_beginner_glossary.md#g079) F32 values, or certified [function-table](APPENDIX_beginner_glossary.md#g074) [bit patterns](APPENDIX_beginner_glossary.md#g057) → per-site F32 operations → F16 [RN-even](APPENDIX_beginner_glossary.md#g059), tail [RZ16](APPENDIX_beginner_glossary.md#g060), or continued retention in F32; [E4](APPENDIX_beginner_glossary.md#g055) is published separately from half via RN-even/[satfinite](APPENDIX_beginner_glossary.md#g062).
- How it is computed: First look up the entry and exit formats in the [site table](#sites), then determine input/output [flush-to-zero](APPENDIX_beginner_glossary.md#g068), retention, or rejection according to the [FTZ table](#ftz-paths), and finally execute the specified conversion; one cannot simply set a single uniform FTZ mode for the entire process.
- Numeric rules: Separate F32 multiplies and adds each apply RN32, and only explicit [FMA](APPENDIX_beginner_glossary.md#g063) fuses; FTZ, RZ, half RN-even, [texture](APPENDIX_beginner_glossary.md#g027) half midpoints rounding away from zero, and E4 satfinite are distinct contracts.
- Why: Coordinates, function arguments, and display colors retain their own auxiliary precision; [matrices](APPENDIX_beginner_glossary.md#g051)/[vector norms](APPENDIX_beginner_glossary.md#g043) are still published according to their low-precision interfaces. The training or hardware motivations for choosing these precision and fusion boundaries are unknown.
- How we know: Current CPU source code, saved named hardware/software records, and this round's small real C++ examples are labeled separately; the existence of source code, passing legacy gates, and general hardware semantics do not upgrade one another.
- Next down: This topic has reached the bottom; [conversion formats](#formats), [sites](#sites), [FTZ](#ftz-paths), [portable implementation / compilation](#portable), [real conversion example](#worked-example), [evidence scope](#evidence).

The source function names fa/fm/ff and tn_add/tn_mul/tn_div/tn_fma are implementation identifiers; the mathematical interface in this text is uniformly written as F32+, F32×, F32/, and F32_FMA, without changing the arithmetic or acceptance domains of these functions.

## Three Subnormal Types and Four Publication Rules Must Not Be Mixed

An F32 [encoding](APPENDIX_beginner_glossary.md#g057) consists of a sign, an 8-bit [exponent](APPENDIX_beginner_glossary.md#g058), and a 23-bit mantissa. Only an exponent of 0 with a nonzero mantissa is an F32 subnormal, with magnitude m·2⁻¹⁴⁹; the smallest normal number is 2⁻¹²⁶. The smallest F16 subnormal is 2⁻²⁴, which becomes an **F32 normal number** after expanding to F32; E4 subnormals in turn have their own exponent/mantissa definitions. [Format source](L4_half_arith.md#format), [E4 format](L4_e4m3_and_k32.md#e4m3-format).

| Notation / conversion | Exact meaning |
|---|---|
| RN32, RN16 | Round-to-nearest-even published as binary32 and binary16 respectively; F32 widens exactly to binary64 before RN16, introducing no extra numeric rounding |
| RZ16 | F32 published toward zero as half; the tail stage first calls the real half_round and then steps back by one half encoding toward zero when the magnitude of the RN result exceeds the input |
| FTZ | Subnormal inputs / tiny outputs of an F32 operation become [signed zero](APPENDIX_beginner_glossary.md#g070) according to its contract; not equal to RZ in format conversion |
| [Q8](APPENDIX_beginner_glossary.md#g061) / satfinite | half→E4, RN-even with finite saturation; does not replace domain checks or [NaN](APPENDIX_beginner_glossary.md#g069) rejection of the preceding F32/half operation |
| sat / texture half publication | sat clamps to [0,1] and normalizes zero according to the current tail code; texture-filtering half rounds midpoints away from zero, and neither is E4 satfinite |

[Position biases](APPENDIX_beginner_glossary.md#g052) serve directly as the half matrix [seed](APPENDIX_beginner_glossary.md#g042), and most [residual](APPENDIX_beginner_glossary.md#g017) [scales](APPENDIX_beginner_glossary.md#g031) are also consumed as half; the query scale is F32 in the resource and undergoes RN16 first before being used in half multiplication. The Q/K path here has no extra additive β; F32 addends in coordinate offsets and display controls are handled at their respective sites. Model coefficient values are not listed; [host](APPENDIX_beginner_glossary.md#g096) F32 controls in the parameters are likewise read only according to their named layout, without writing a value from a single capture as a general algorithmic constant.

The configuration-time half function tables in B0/TIN already incorporate the corresponding F32 table value → RN16, so runtime execution may fetch half directly; one cannot claim from the latter that no underlying F32 auxiliary interface exists. The score affine transform in split16 is a half FMA and cannot be merged into the two-step F32 affine transform of B0/TIN. [Specific differences](L3_attention_softmax.md#window-attention).

**Where F32 accumulation actually occurs** is in auxiliary chains such as the history weighted values and their denominators in B0/B70. [QMMA](APPENDIX_beginner_glossary.md#g065) matrix feedback per K32 is F16, and the norm sum of squares, [window](APPENDIX_beginner_glossary.md#g015) denominator, and ViT same-address partial sums are likewise published according to their respective half trees/orders; they cannot be replaced by an F32 GEMM/summation converted to half once at the end. Software helpers using binary64 to carry the half reference or integers to carry the matrix rule do not mean the network has an F32 accumulator. [Matrix contract](L3_qmma_e4m3.md#qmma-e4m3), [ordered half merge](L4_half_arith.md#atomic-add).

### Tininess Is Determined Before Rounding

Let m=2⁻¹²⁶. For synthetic inputs a=m and b=1−2⁻²⁴, the exact product m(1−2⁻²⁴) is less than m, yet lies right at the midpoint that rounds to m. If RN32 were applied first and the result were only checked afterward for being subnormal, m would be retained; under pre-rounding tininess, zero should be obtained. Across 5120 sampled cases from saved saturating F32 fused multiply-add sites, this mechanism occurs in two cases, supporting pre-rounding determination at that site; it does not prove all nonzero addends, all operations, or all hardware versions. [Original sampling scope](../../../experiments/b1-ftz-tininess-check-r01-20261012-claude/RESULT_ZH.md#L1).

In the current code, the product is exact in binary64, and the fused sum uses [TwoSum](APPENDIX_beginner_glossary.md#g098) to save the high and low parts; when the high part is exactly ±m, the direction of the low part is also inspected, avoiding a check based solely on the already-rounded F32 result. The original 2⁻¹²⁰ guard remains in place, so calling the full fm/ff on the small example above rejects; this round verified this directly. Unit evidence for the historical fix draft and its unmerged / unrun-launch-gate status at the time remain in the [original report](../../../experiments/parity-cpp-defect-fix-newseal-r01-20261012-codex/RESULT_ZH.md#L1); this page takes the currently read source code as its object and does not transfer the status of that legacy draft to all later seals.

## How the CPU Reproduces This, and Which Environmental Premises Remain

1. **Explicit bit handling.** Input FTZ checks the F32 exponent field and retains only the sign bit; MUFU obtains specified outputs via certified raw32 / integer codecs; half_round publishes in software by power-of-two grid, remainder, and even mantissa, with RZ then stepping back by magnitude. They do not rely on enabling host FTZ/DAZ to obtain results.
2. **Preserving publication points.** Add/multiply/divide and noise multiplication in B0 use volatile float to preserve RN32 boundaries; the original score formula in TIN preserves two intermediate float values. volatile constrains only these publications and does not by itself provide FTZ or a correctness proof for arbitrary precision.
3. **Specified fusion; extra fusion prohibited.** B0 coordinates and 5-tap, as well as tail ff, use explicit std::fma(float,float,float); independent multiplies and adds remain independent, and fa implements addition via ff(a,1,b). The binary64→F16 two-stage behavior of half std::fma belongs to [another contract](L4_half_arith.md#rounding).
4. **Strict compilation and environment.** The recorded GCC/Clang recipe is -O2, -fno-fast-math, -ffp-contract=off; MSVC uses /fp:strict and the corresponding strict recipe. [plan_format:25–28](https://github.com/Ashley-QwQ/GlassBox-NR/blob/e4a9b4214c723015ff4a99d73f13aa9a4a8ed1ec/devkit/plan_format.py#L25). The TIN unary generator also checks FE_TONEAREST and 4 B / 8 B IEEE float/double while rejecting fast-math, [unary_tables:5–6, 29–33](https://github.com/Ashley-QwQ/GlassBox-NR/blob/e4a9b4214c723015ff4a99d73f13aa9a4a8ed1ec/cpp/chain_tin_optm/unary_tables_optb2.inc#L29). Reassociation, implicit contraction, or replacing table values with libm is prohibited.
5. **Guards and unknowns retained as-is.** Current tail tiny results, nonfinite/overflow values, ViT helper multiplications, and function-table domains each have independent rejection conditions. Ordinary host float operations still presuppose IEEE round-to-nearest-even semantics; when porting, gradual underflow on paths that must be preserved needs to be maintained—external FTZ/DAZ must not be allowed to corrupt them, nor can one claim that arbitrary B0 tiny arguments are already equivalent under arbitrary global flags. The proper approach is bitwise handling or executing/rejecting within the proven normal range, rather than substituting global control-word changes for operator contracts.

This round did not alter compilation configurations or any global rounding/FTZ/DAZ flags. The strict recipes above are the source-code / existing identity contracts; the actual compiler, flags, and source SHAs for this round's small example are listed separately in [result JSON](../skewer/examples/fp32_ftz_r04e.result.json), without treating successful local compilation as acceptance across all CPU/compiler ports.

## Real C++ Boundary: At a half Subnormal Midpoint, RN and RZ Differ

The [executable script](../skewer/examples/fp32_ftz_r04e.py) extracts verbatim the pure [scalar](APPENDIX_beginner_glossary.md#g076) functions rz, ftz, and their underlying half_value/half_round from current outtail, compiles them, and runs them directly; it imports no network, reads no model / network inputs / targets, and runs no [GPU](APPENDIX_beginner_glossary.md#g078). The helper namespace provides only the same bit-copy interface; function bodies are preserved verbatim, and the full identity and stdout are in the [run record](../skewer/examples/fp32_ftz_r04e.result.json).

The central input raw32=0x33c00000, i.e., 3·2⁻²⁵, is an **F32 normal number** lying right at the midpoint between the two half subnormal values 2⁻²⁴ and 2⁻²³. RN16 selects the even encoding 0x0002; the real rz first obtains this RN result, finds that its magnitude exceeds the input, and steps back to 0x0001. The result of RZ is still a half subnormal and is not cleared by F32 FTZ.

| Synthetic F32 encoding | Original half_round RN16 | Original rz RZ16 | RZ output little-endian bytes |
|---|---|---|---|
| 33800000 (2⁻²⁴) | 0001 | 0001 | 01 00 |
| 33c00000 (3·2⁻²⁵) | 0002 | 0001 | 01 00 |
| b3c00000 (−3·2⁻²⁵) | 8002 | 8001 | 01 80 |

The negative example tests only the pure conversion function; actual display RGB also has [0,1] clamping, so a network path for negative RGB cannot be inferred from this. The same small program also verifies that the smallest positive and negative F32 subnormals yield +0/−0 through the real ftz, and that the tiny example above is rejected by the full fm/ff guard. It checks only these synthetic interfaces and performs no new full-raw32 hardware gate for RZ or image-chain run.
